# Feature Roadmap

This document tracks the implemented and planned features for Activity.next.

## Current Features

### Core

- ✅ **ActivityPub federation** — Send and receive activities with other Fediverse servers
- ✅ **Notes** — Create, receive, edit, and delete posts
- ✅ **Profile federation** — Profile edits are sent to followers and relays as `Update(Person)` (only when something the federated profile carries changed), deleting an account sends `Delete(actor)`, and a remote `Update(Person)` refreshes the stored profile right away from the actor's own server. The actor document carries `manuallyApprovesFollowers`, the `discoverable` and `indexable` opt-ins, and the profile links as Mastodon `PropertyValue` attachments; local aliases are supported via `GET/POST/DELETE /api/v1/accounts/aliases` (up to 10; alsoKnownAs is set through the aliases API; movedTo is emitted when set, but nothing sets it on a local actor yet — outgoing Move is not implemented); a remote actor's `movedTo` and `alsoKnownAs` are stored and the Mastodon Account shows `moved` when the target is already known, and an inbound `Move` from another server moves your accepted local follows of that account, and the lists that hold it, to the new remote or local account (when the target account lists the old one as an alias, at most one verified Move per account every 7 days; local followers of a local target are repointed directly; account notes, blocks and mutes are carried over; a blocked account or domain is skipped, and an existing follow of the new account only gets the options copied), and notes send and receive the "mark media as sensitive" flag
- ✅ **Replies** — Threaded conversation support, including on-demand fetching of full remote reply threads when viewing a remote status
- ✅ **Combined self-replies in the timeline** — A run of quick replies by one author to their own posts shows as one post: one header with the time span, a thread rail with a "2 of 4 · 15m" label per later post, other people's short text replies tucked in as small cards, and one action bar (Reply goes to the latest post, everything else to the first). Posts more than 10 minutes apart or with a different visibility stay separate, each later part and each tucked-in reply keeps its own ⋯ menu, and the combined post's ⋯ menu has **Show as separate posts** (kept for that row until the page is reloaded). An edited later post keeps its own edit-history button. The post's own page combines too (next bullet).
- ✅ **Combined self-replies on the post page** — Opening any post of a run shows the run of the author's quick self-replies around it as one run under one header with the time span, the thread rail and "i of n" labels, built from the ancestors, the opened post and the author's direct self-reply chain with the same rules (short text-only replies from others tucked in). Unlike the timeline, each part keeps its own small action row with its own counts, parts are not clamped, and the opened part is marked (tint, orange dot) and scrolled into view. Other runs in the same chain get their own headers. A reply to an earlier part that cannot tuck in stays under Replies with a "Replying to i of n" line; "Replies (N)" and "No replies yet" count only replies outside the run. Runs over 6 parts collapse the middle, and **Show as separate posts** is in part 1's menu. On a fitness activity page the athlete's follow-ups render as a rail under the activity's action bar from part 2, outside the replies count, paging and Hide/Show toggle.
- ✅ **Federated Audio and Event objects** — Funkwhale tracks and Mobilizon/Gancio events from other servers are stored as posts, with the title, the event's time and place, and the track's audio attached
- ✅ **Image and video attachments** — Upload and display images and video in posts (with automatic preview frames and blurhash)
- ✅ **Boost / Repost** — Share other users' posts (with undo)
- ✅ **Like / Favorite** — React to posts (with undo)
- ✅ **Polls** — Create, vote on, and view poll results (single and multiple choice)
- ✅ **Quote posts (Mastodon 4.5)** — Quote another post with the full FEP-044f consent handshake (`QuoteRequest` → `Accept` + a hosted `QuoteAuthorization` stamp; revocation is a `Delete` of the stamp), a per-post quote-approval policy (`public` / `followers` / `nobody`) with a per-account default, and inbound interop with Fedibird (`quoteUri`) and Misskey (`_misskey_quote`) quotes (rendered as unapproved until a stamp verifies)
- ✅ **Multiple profiles per account & account menu** — Create and switch between multiple handles/profiles under one account (e.g., `@user@domain.tld` and `@ride@domain.tld`) via the unified account menu at the bottom of the sidebar (and the collapsed rail avatar or mobile drawer). The account row serves both single-profile and multi-profile accounts: identity header, View profile, Edit profile, profile switching list with pending-deletion notices and cancellation, "Add another profile", plus Account & security and Sign out
- ✅ **Multi-domain support** — Different domains for different actors
- ✅ **Account blocks** — Block or unblock remote accounts and list blocked accounts
- ✅ **Domain blocks** — Block or unblock an entire remote domain and list your blocked domains via `/api/v1/domain_blocks`; blocking hides that domain's posts (and boosts) from your timelines, is reflected in `domain_blocking` on relationships, and severs follows in both directions
- ✅ **Follow requests** — Review, authorize, and reject follow requests
- ✅ **Custom emoji** — Instance-defined custom emoji with a sticker/emoji picker in the post box, and custom emoji shortcode (`:shortcode:`) support across user display names, profile bios, profile metadata fields, content warnings, poll choices, and media attachment descriptions in the web UI, composer preview, Mastodon API, and ActivityPub federation
- ✅ **Profile links** — Up to four label/content fields per local account (a remote account can carry more: as on Mastodon, the first 50 of its `PropertyValue` attachments are kept, each label and content trimmed (`String#trim`) and cut to 2047 characters, and the extra ones sit behind "+N more") under the bio, as on Mastodon: edited on the Edit profile page (Settings → Profile, "Profile fields": only filled fields show as rows, suggestion chips add Location, Pronouns, Website, Email, Strava, Photos or a custom field, rows reorder and remove, each row's icon follows its label and content like the profile's, and the live profile preview beside the form shows the result) or through `update_credentials`, shown on the profile page for local and remote accounts on one line under the bio with an icon each (the first three visible, two on phones, the rest behind "+N more"), federated in the actor document (a URL becomes Mastodon's link markup, anything else is escaped text) and returned as HTML in the Account entity's `fields` (`source.fields` stays raw). A link back is checked in the background as on Mastodon: when the page a link points at has an `<a>` or `<link>` with `rel="me"` to the profile (or the actor id), the field gets a check mark on the profile, `fields[].verified_at` in the Account entity and a "Verified" status on Edit profile, which says "Checking for a link back to your profile…" for a link that was just changed or saved (a changed value starts unverified; an unverified https link is checked again on every save, http links are not verifiable; verification is never federated, each server checks for itself)
- ✅ **Featured hashtags** — Feature hashtags on your profile and manage them in the Featured hashtags section of Edit profile (Settings → Profile), where adding or removing one saves at once
- ✅ **Status translation** — Translate posts into your language via DeepL, LibreTranslate, or an OpenAI-compatible backend
- ✅ **Public & logged-out pages** — Logged-out landing page plus publicly viewable profile and status pages, with design-system error pages (404/500)

### Authentication & OAuth

- ✅ **Local authentication** — Email and password sign-up/sign-in (via [better-auth](https://www.better-auth.com/))
- ✅ **Passkeys** — Register and use passkeys for account authentication
- ✅ **Two-factor authentication** — TOTP-based second-factor login support
- ✅ **OAuth 2.0 provider** — App acts as a full OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect server
- ✅ **OIDC RP-Initiated Logout** — `end_session_endpoint` advertised in discovery; OAuth clients registered with `post_logout_redirect_uris` can drive single logout (sign the user out of this instance, not just the relying party)
- ✅ **OAuth Bearer tokens** — API authentication for third-party clients
- ✅ **Email verification** — Required for new accounts
- ✅ **Password reset** — Reset password via email
- ✅ **Sign out & unsent draft protection** — Explicit sign-out flow (shared between the account menu and Sessions & apps via `SignOutDialog`) confirms sign-out on this device while other devices stay signed in. It inspects local on-device drafts (`countDraftsByState`), distinguishing waiting queued posts from plain drafts: if unsent posts exist, it adds an error alert with counts, a link to review drafts, and a destructive "Sign out and delete" action. Broadcasts `signing-out` across tabs, wipes offline caches (`activities_offline_v1`), and purges on-device drafts only after the server confirms sign-out so failed or offline sign-outs preserve unsent work

### Timelines & Notifications

- ✅ **Main timeline** — Home feed with posts from followed accounts
- ✅ **Favorites page** — Browse posts you've favorited
- ✅ **List timelines** — Per-list timelines honoring replies policy, exclusive lists, and block/mute/keyword filtering. As in Mastodon, you can add yourself to your own list to see your own posts in it alongside its members', an exclusive list never hides your own posts from Home, and direct messages never appear in a list — yours or anyone else's. Adding yourself backfills your 200 most recent posts other than direct messages, where Mastodon backfills none; replies the list's replies policy hides still count toward the 200
- ✅ **Notifications** — Like, follow, mention, reblog, follow request, quote (someone quoted your post), quote-update (a post you quoted was edited), and emoji reaction (someone reacted to your post, served as the Pleroma `pleroma:emoji_reaction` type) notifications
- ✅ **Notification grouping** — Group similar notifications together
- ✅ **Email notifications** — Configurable email alerts for each notification type, including fitness activity imports (managed under **Settings → Notifications**, with "Change email address" linking to Account & security at `/account/email`)
- ✅ **Push notifications** — Web Push subscriptions with VAPID configuration
- ✅ **Unread count** — Badge showing the unread notification count on the Notifications row of the sidebar, the collapsed rail and the mobile navigation drawer (never on the mobile menu button)
- ✅ **Customizable navigation** — Hide, restore, and reorder the sidebar per account. Any item that is not pinned (Timeline, Search, Lists, Notifications, and Settings always show; Profile replaces Account & security and can be hidden under More) can be hidden from the `⋯` menu on its own row, which tucks it under a **More** group at the end of the navigation rather than taking it away; one click puts it back. Profile links to the current actor's profile page and is active only on your own profile; Account & security and Sign out are located in the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar. **Settings → Sidebar** (`/settings/sidebar`; `/settings/navigation` redirects here) manages the whole list — reorder by dragging, by the arrows on each row, or with the arrow keys on its grip, plus a switch per item and a reset — saving as you go through `POST /api/v1/accounts/navigation-preferences` into the actor's settings. The order carries across the desktop sidebar, the tablet collapsed rail, and the mobile navigation drawer (where the unread notification badge is displayed on the Notifications row). Below 768px every signed-in page opens that same drawer from a compact 56px bar — the menu button and one short page title — or, on a profile, from a round menu button floating over the cover; page actions, descriptions, filters and Back stay in the page itself (the timeline's Refresh button is the exception and sits at the end of the bar). Logged-out readers of public profiles, posts, hashtags and shared heatmaps keep the branded top bar with Sign in and (while registration is open) Create account at every width. Admins can additionally turn Fitness, Explore or Messages off for the whole instance — see [Database-backed server settings](environment-variables.md#database-backed-server-settings-env--database--default)

- ✅ **Status emoji reactions** — React to any post with a unicode emoji or an instance custom emoji, separately from favouriting it. Chips under each post show each emoji with its count (yours highlighted), and a react button in the post's action row opens a picker to add your own (on a narrow post it moves into the `⋯` menu); the accounts behind a reaction are available from the API rather than the chips. Reactions federate both ways: inbound from Pleroma/Akkoma (`EmojiReact`) and the Misskey family (a `Like` carrying `content`/`_misskey_reaction`), outbound as a Misskey-style `Like` that vanilla Mastodon shows as a favourite. Served on every status as `pleroma.emoji_reactions` and `reactions`, with `PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/pleroma/statuses/:id/reactions/:emoji` (plus glitch-soc `react`/`unreact` aliases)

- ✅ **Link preview cards** — A post containing a link shows a card with the page's title, description, publisher and thumbnail, the way Mastodon, Bluesky and other social apps do. The server fetches each page once through the SSRF-guarded outbound fetcher, caches the result per URL (shared by every post linking the same page, refreshed weekly, failures suppressed for an hour), and serves it as the Mastodon `Status.card` so third-party clients render it too. Cards are fetched for remote posts as well as local ones, with a random delay so a widely-shared link is not hit by every receiving server at once. The card yields to media, a quoted post or a fitness activity when a post has one. A link to a **YouTube video** shows the video itself instead of the usual card: a thumbnail with a play button that swaps in the embedded player when you press it, so none of the player — around a megabyte of code, and the tracking that comes with it — is loaded until you choose to watch. The thumbnail itself is still fetched from YouTube's image host up front, without a referrer, the same way any other card's thumbnail is. The player is always framed from the cookie-light `youtube-nocookie.com` host. Admins can turn fetching off under **Admin → Network → Link previews**

### Storage & Media

- ✅ **SQL database support** — SQLite (`better-sqlite3`) and PostgreSQL (`pg`)
- ✅ **Media upload** — Upload images and video to local filesystem, S3, or S3-compatible object storage
- ✅ **Blurhash & smart focal points** — Automatic server-side blurhash placeholder generation and saliency-based smart focal point detection on uploaded images and video preview frames, with client-overridable focal points, inbound ActivityPub focal point/blurhash parsing, and timeline blurhash placeholder canvas rendering. Every path that attaches media carries them — composing, editing a posted status (including a focal point moved from a Mastodon client), route map images, and Strava photo and archive imports — and both image and video attachments serialise `meta.focus`
- ✅ **Automated alt text** — Generate accessibility descriptions for uploaded images and videos from a configured OpenAI-compatible vision endpoint (`ACTIVITIES_ALT_TEXT_*`) when the client sends none, as well as route descriptions for fitness activity route map images when enabled in the actor's Fitness Settings (off by default). Video descriptions come from the representative preview frame, which is more likely than frame 0 to show the scene instead of a black or blank opening; a vision or preview-extraction failure degrades to an upload or activity with no generated description rather than failing the upload or processing. Storing the video's own poster frame is still required and fatal if the clip cannot be decoded
- ✅ **Media management** — Browse, view, and delete uploaded media from the settings page with storage usage display
- ✅ **Media details & EXIF** — Uploaded photos carry optional subject (name, scientific name, category), taken-at, camera and lens gear, exposure, place and gallery membership. EXIF is read once at upload (stored copies carry none of it) to pre-fill taken-at, gear, exposure and a GPS place; owners edit everything through `PUT /api/v1/media/:id`, request generated alt text via `POST /api/v1/media/:id/describe`, and tune defaults (gallery membership, place precision, auto alt text, whether media may be posted without a description, subject hashtags, gear visibility) under `/api/v1/gallery/settings`; Settings → Media exposes auto alt text, the empty-description rule, subject hashtags and the default place precision (new GPS-tagged uploads disclose nothing publicly by default, `hidden`; coordinates are still stored for the owner, who can pick a precision per photo). The lightbox keeps the photo at one size cap, offers an albums button in the header for your own photos, and hides the alt text and public details until you press **Details** in its header; they then show in an overlay over the bottom of the stage, for the photo on screen. The overlay stays open while you move between photos, **Esc** or a click on the backdrop closes it first, and the button is hidden for a photo with nothing to show. Public viewers get a privacy-trimmed view from `GET /api/v1/gallery/media/:mediaId/details`
- ✅ **Photo editor** — Owners can edit their own photos from the media details dialog: crop with aspect presets, rotate, flip and straighten, plus exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, white balance, vibrance, saturation, texture, clarity and vignette, local adjustments on up to four masks (select the subject with an on-device model, its background, or paint with a brush), with a live RGB histogram that flags shadow and highlight clipping, undo/redo and keyboard shortcuts, and a Presets tab offering curated look presets with a Strength slider. Edits run in the browser (WebGL), which uploads the result; the server stores it as a WebP copy beside the untouched upload, and revert restores the original.
- ✅ **Animated GIF and GIFV playback** — Detect and play animated GIFs and GIFV attachments in timeline statuses and media lightbox according to the reader's saved GIF autoplay preference, with accessible manual playback controls, reduced motion support, and Mastodon API parity (`type: "gifv"`)
- ✅ **Gallery** — An owner-only `/gallery` section (Subjects, All media, Albums, Map, Life list, Gear, Privacy) over photos with media details. Subjects group by name with first/last seen and show how many countries the photos cover; the map clusters photos by place and counts countries; the life list says where each species was first seen; a subject page shows its taxonomy path, a link to GBIF, the scientific-name hashtag to follow and where it was seen; the life list tracks first sightings; gear pages show photos, videos and places and the photos taken with each camera or lens. **Gallery → All media** (`/gallery/media`, which replaces Recent; `/gallery/recent` redirects there) lists every photo and video you have posted or added, defaulting to date taken (newest first; also date taken oldest first, or recently uploaded), including those you have switched off from your gallery, which carry a _Hidden_ badge only you see. A **Show** menu next to Category narrows it to _Everything_ (the default), _In gallery_, _Hidden from gallery_, _Not posted_ or _Not in any album_; the selection is kept across filters. Filter and sort controls let you narrow by Category, Show, Species, Place, Date taken (this month, this year, specific year/month, or custom date range), Activity, and Sort. A count line under the filters shows "N photos" with a "Clear filters" action. When no photos match, an empty state displays "No photos match these filters", an active filters summary, a "Remove <last filter>" button, and "Clear filters". On phones, tapping filters opens a full-screen bottom sheet with searchable lists and a live "Show N" button. **Add** (or drag files onto the page on a desktop) puts photos and videos in your gallery without a post: an _Add to gallery_ dialog uploads them as it opens (up to 40 at a time), reads their date, camera and place, suggests subjects as the composer does, lets you set the subject, place and gear of one and apply it to all, and keeps them when you press **Add N to gallery**. If a post that used one of these photos is later deleted, the photo goes back to _Only you_ (Not posted) rather than being deleted. Add is offered only when media storage is configured. Only you can see what you add, marked _Only you_, in the Gallery, the map, Subjects, Life list, Gear and your albums, never on your profile or to anyone else and never federated, until you post it: **Post them** in the toast that follows, **Post** in Select mode, or **Post…** in Edit details opens Home with those photos attached (up to the instance's attachment limit per post). A photo you have added and not posted can be deleted (**Delete** in Select mode or Edit details, after a confirmation); a posted photo never offers Delete. Cancelling the dialog removes what it uploaded. Open a photo and tap **Edit** beside _Details_ (or **Select**, pick up to 40 photos and choose **Edit details**) to change its subject, place, gear and _Show in my gallery_ without opening the composer; **Edit** is also in the viewer of your own posts on the timeline, your profile and the post page. A posted photo's alt text is saved as an edit of the post, like Mastodon, so followers see it as edited (one edit for all the photos of a post you change together). Gallery → Privacy controls place precision, hidden locations, gear visibility and whether the map and life list are public, and whether the place of a threatened species is hidden. Visitors get a profile Gallery tab, shown when they may see gallery photos or open at least one album, with the subviews the owner has made public, plus Albums when the viewer can open one.
- ✅ **Gallery albums** — Group your own gallery photos into albums under **Gallery → Albums** (`/gallery/albums`): a card for each album with a three-photo collage, its photo count and date range, All / Public / Private filters, and a dashed _New album_ card. The create dialog takes a title (up to 120 characters), a description (up to 1,000), Public or Private, and a _From gallery_ picker (listing every photo you posted or added, with caption, "Not in gallery" and "In N albums" badges; filter by Species, Place, Date taken, Activity, a "Not in an album" toggle, and Sort; dateless photos show an "Uploaded <date>" caption; the count line under filters shows "N photos · K already in this album" with "Clear filters"; empty results show "No photos match these filters" with an active filters summary, "Remove <last filter>", and "Clear filters"; on phones, filters open in a bottom sheet with full-screen lists and a "Show N" button; the first photo picked is the cover). Nothing is saved until **Create album**. An album page shows a hero cover with the title and date range, the description, a facts line (photos, species, places and countries, days), species chips, a sort menu (taken newest or oldest first, or recently added) and the photos, which open in the lightbox. **Edit** shows _Remove_ and _Set as cover_ on each photo (removing never deletes the photo or its post); **Add photos** (which offers Suggestions, with Use adding with Undo, marks photos already in the album, and includes the same picker filters, count line, and empty state) and **Delete** (after a confirmation) complete the page. An album never holds more than 2,000 photos, an actor no more than 200 albums. The numbers are computed from what a visitor could see (a private album shows its owner the same figures), so a photo from a followers-only post or a deleted post adds nothing public (a photo outside the gallery counts publicly when its post is public), and a threatened species' place adds no place or country; the owner is told how many places are hidden, on the album page and on its card. Albums hold only your own photos, are not federated, and deleting a photo or a post takes it out of every album. **Share link** on the album page copies the public address (`/@user@domain/albums/<id>`); it is disabled, with the reason written under the title, for a private album, and a public album with no public photo (its photos are all followers-only or deleted) warns that the link shows a not-found page to anyone signed out or not following you. When you open your own private or empty album at its public address, a small note, seen only by you, says visitors cannot open it and links to the album page in your gallery. **Suggestions** are worked out for you when you open **Gallery → Albums**: a _Suggestions_ tab next to _From gallery_ in the create dialog lists trips (photos taken over days, with no gap of more than 3 days, at least 8 photos), species you have photographed at least 5 times, and days on which you recorded an activity and took at least 5 photos, newest first. Days are the dates the album will show (UTC); an activity counts for a day when it falls on that date or, when none does, on the date (in your time zone) you took one of the photos. A "N suggested albums" chip and a _Suggested_ count on the Albums tab, and a _See suggestions_ button when you have no albums yet, open the dialog on that tab. **Use N** selects those photos (Use adds with Undo) and fills in the title, the photos can be reviewed and trimmed (filter them by species, place and date; **Select all** adds to your picks), and nothing is saved until **Create album**. A suggestion that one of your albums already holds in full is not offered again. A suggested title speaks only for the photos a visitor could see, and names a place only when all of them show that one place; a hidden location, a hidden or failed place check, a threatened species, a photo with no place or photos at more than one place make it a date title ("Trip, 12–19 Sep 2026") instead. When no photo of a suggestion is visible to a visitor the title is plain ("Trip", "Species series", "Day out"), and a day says "Activity day" only when one of its activities is on a post a visitor can read. Suggestions are not stored and are for you alone (`GET /api/v1/gallery/albums/suggestions`). The owner API is `POST`/`GET /api/v1/gallery/albums`, `GET`/`PATCH`/`DELETE /api/v1/gallery/albums/:id` and `GET`/`POST`/`DELETE /api/v1/gallery/albums/:id/items` (see [the API notes](mastodon-api-compatibility.md)).
- ✅ **Public albums** — A public album opens for anyone at `/@user@domain/albums/<id>`, with no sign-in: the cover, title, date range, description, facts line, species chips, a sort menu and the photos, which open in the lightbox with public details only. Each viewer sees the photos of the posts they could read on the profile (public and unlisted for everyone; followers-only for followers; never direct posts, deleted posts or photos outside the gallery) and the counts, date range and cover are computed from those alone; the place of a threatened, unchecked or hidden-location photo is left out exactly as on the profile. A private album, one that does not exist and one with nothing this viewer can see are the same not-found page. The page has no owner controls, no conservation data and no hint of what is hidden. Link previews (Open Graph and Twitter cards) are built for a logged-out visitor whoever asks: title, a description with the visible photo count, dates and country, and a cover that is one of those visible photos. The profile Gallery tab gets an **Albums** subview, offered only to a viewer who can open at least one album. The API is `GET /api/v1/accounts/:id/gallery/albums` and `GET /api/v1/accounts/:id/gallery/albums/:albumId` (see [the API notes](mastodon-api-compatibility.md)).
- ✅ **Add to an album from anywhere** — Albums can be filled without leaving the photo. The media details dialog (the composer's _Edit details_) has an **Albums** section for your own photos: a chip for each album that holds the photo (opening it in a new tab) and an **Add to album** menu, a checkbox for each of your albums. Ticking or unticking saves at once and shows a message with **Undo**, and it is not part of **Save details** (the dialog says so; nothing in it changes what Save details sends). **New album with this photo** opens the create dialog with the photo already chosen. The same menu is the **In N albums** button in the lightbox's top bar (between Details and Edit), for your own photos on your Gallery pages (All media, a subject, a piece of gear, an album) and in your own posts, whether you open them from the home timeline, a thread or the post's own page. It is not offered from the Media and Gallery tabs of a profile page. In **Gallery → All media**, **Select** turns the tiles into checkboxes and a bar at the bottom shows _N selected_ with **Select all loaded**, **Clear**, **Edit details** and **Add to album**: pick an album (or **New album with these photos**) and the photos are added in batches of 100. It tells you how many were added, how many were already there, which could not be added, and, if the album reaches 2,000 photos part way, how far it got. A fresh upload in compose can be added to albums, and will automatically appear in the album once the post is published. An older photo that is not posted or added in Gallery cannot be added (the menu says so) but can still be taken out (with no Undo, since putting it back would be refused). On an album's own page, changing that album from the lightbox button refreshes the page (photos, facts and count) when the viewer closes. The menus work with the keyboard and a screen reader (every album's checkbox has its own name, changes are announced), and have 40px targets on touch screens.
- ✅ **Smart subjects, threatened-species hiding and place names** — With an image model configured for alt text, the media details dialog suggests what a photo shows (up to three candidates and a group such as "Bird?"), checks them against the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy, and fills the species, scientific name, category and taxonomy path when the owner picks one. Suggestions are never applied on their own: a subject, and its hashtags (`#CommonKingfisher #AlcedoAtthis`), exist only once the owner saves. GBIF also supplies the IUCN Red List status, which is **never shown to anyone but the owner** and only drives a privacy rule: with **Gallery → Privacy → Hide the place for threatened species** on (the default), the place of a photo whose species is Vulnerable, Endangered or Critically Endangered is withheld from everyone else, over any precision, on the lightbox, the gallery, the map and every country count. The rule **fails closed**: a place is shown only once a lookup confirms the subject is one species that is not threatened. A common name GBIF cannot pin to a single species, a genus or family, or a name it cannot match keeps the place hidden, and the owner is told "We couldn't confirm the species" and can pick the species to show it; on a server with lookups switched off or unreachable the place of every photo with a species name is hidden from others. GPS uploads also get a place name and country from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, sent only the roughly 5 km cell around the photo, never the exact point. Admins can switch species lookups and place names off independently under **Admin → Network**, or point them at their own services with `ACTIVITIES_GALLERY_GBIF_ENDPOINT` and `ACTIVITIES_GALLERY_NOMINATIM_ENDPOINT` (see [environment variables](environment-variables.md)); `scripts/maintenance/backfillGalleryLookups.ts` resolves media uploaded earlier.
- ✅ **Profile media gallery** — Browse a user's public media posts from profile tabs
- ✅ **Profile tab visibility** — The Replies, Media, Gallery and Fitness tabs of a profile can be hidden (Posts always stays) with the switches in the Profile tabs section of Edit profile; the setting is saved with `PATCH /api/v1/accounts/profile`, applies to every viewer and is never federated. Hiding a tab does not make anything private: locations stay governed by Fitness privacy and Gallery privacy, which the section links to

One Save (a sticky bar on desktop, Cancel / Save in the top bar on phones) sends only what changed as JSON and shows a "Profile saved" message; field errors from the server show under their field; leaving with unsaved changes asks "Discard changes?". When the editor is at least 860px wide (a container query: from `lg` the Settings rail takes width), a live preview of the profile and a "Set in Settings" card (Who can follow you linking to `/settings/privacy`, Fitness privacy linking to `/fitness/privacy`, Gallery privacy linking to `/gallery/privacy`) sit beside the form.

  - **Preferences**: **Appearance** (`/settings/appearance` — device theme and post line limits), **Posting & reading** (`/settings/preferences` — default visibility, quote policy, language, sensitive content), **Notifications** (`/settings/notifications` — push and email), **Sidebar** (`/settings/sidebar` — show, hide, and reorder navigation; `/settings/navigation` permanently redirects here), **Media & AI** (`/settings/media` — alt text, subject detection, AI enhance)
  - **Privacy & safety**: **Privacy** (`/settings/privacy` — manual follower approval, plus links to Fitness and Gallery privacy), **Blocked accounts** (`/settings/blocks`), **Muted accounts** (`/settings/mutes`), **Filters** (`/settings/filters`)
  - **Delete this profile** (`/settings/delete`) — remove profile and posts from this server
  - **Account & security** footer link (`/account`) — cross-links to credentials shared across your profiles

- ✅ **Fitness file storage** — Upload .fit, .gpx, and .tcx activity files
- ✅ **Fitness activity processing** — Parse GPS tracks and metrics from uploaded .fit, .gpx, and .tcx files
- ✅ **Fitness activity display** — Show route maps, activity statistics, analysis graphs, and device info in posts
- ✅ **Fitness overview** — A training calendar in the viewer's own time zone, so a late-night run lands on the day it was ridden rather than its UTC date. Year to date by default, with This month, Last 12 months, a historical-year chooser and a custom range (at least 7 days; This month and Year to date are exempt). Totals, a per-activity-type breakdown and the calendar (shaded by count, distance or duration) share the range; selecting a day lists that day's activities, and an untyped activity is counted as its own group. A failed load shows an error with Retry rather than an empty calendar
- ✅ **Pluggable fitness map provider** — Choose `apple` (MapKit JS + Apple Maps Snapshots), `mapbox` (Mapbox GL JS + Static Images API), or `osm` (keyless MapLibre / OpenFreeMap) with `ACTIVITIES_FITNESS_MAP_PROVIDER`; missing or invalid credentials fall back to `osm`. Every Apple-rendered map — interactive and static alike — draws the muted standard basemap, so routes and heat runs stay the most prominent thing on it. Stored route-map images keep the style they were generated with until the user runs **Regenerate maps** (`POST /api/v1/fitness/general/regenerate-maps`). With the `apple` provider, per-activity route-map images are rendered by Apple Web Snapshots; a heatmap embed image with more segments than Apple's overlay ceiling allows is offered the coarser stored geometry instead, and falls back to the built-in SVG heatmap renderer only when that does not fit either
- ✅ **Fitness route heatmaps** — Per-region master/detail heatmaps by actor and region (aggregated across all activities and all time), rendered on an interactive map through the configured map provider (Apple MapKit JS, Mapbox GL JS, or keyless MapLibre / OpenFreeMap), with live generation progress, per-heatmap cancel/retry/remove, inline region renaming, and shareable/embeddable views (iframe + image). An all-activities, all-time heatmap is drawn from a **tile pyramid**, so zooming in reveals street-level detail rather than the same simplified outline at every scale, and a road ridden many times reads hotter than one ridden once — brighter and thicker on the interactive maps, stronger and more yellow (the heat ramp's orange towards its yellow) in the static share image. Heatmaps filtered to a single sport or a single year keep the single-fidelity rendering — the tiles carry no sport and no date, so they cannot answer a filtered view
- ✅ **Gear tracking** — Track bikes and shoes, and the components fitted to a bike, with lifetime distance derived from the activities they are used on rather than stored. A bike's page switches between its components and a feed of its activities, rendered as the posts they were published as. Default sports attribute new activities automatically — including imported ones, which never carry gear across from Strava — retiring keeps a gear's history without offering it for new activities, and optional distance thresholds send a service reminder once per crossing. Every gear — a bike, a pair of shoes or a device — can carry a link to the manufacturer's product page, shown on its own page — see [Fitness File Storage](fitness-file-storage.md#gear-tracking)
- ✅ **Recording devices** — The head unit or watch each activity was recorded on becomes gear of its own, created automatically from what the file recorded and listed beside the bikes and shoes with an activity count, a first-used date, an editable name, a product page like any other gear, and a feed of the activities it recorded. The device name on a post links to its page for the owner; everyone else sees the manufacturer. Devices carry no components, sports or distance total, and existing history is linked by `scripts/fitness/backfillFitnessDevices.ts` — see [Fitness File Storage](fitness-file-storage.md#gear-tracking)
- ✅ **Strava import** — Import activities through Strava OAuth/webhooks and uploaded Strava archive ZIP files
- ✅ **Fitness import resilience** — Recover stuck/orphaned imports, resumable Strava archive retries, same-ride upload merging, and per-file retry from the UI; repair scripts cover legacy imports
- ✅ **Fitness privacy locations** — Trim the start and finish of a route around configured locations on route maps and route data, keeping the exposed middle as one unbroken line. Route heatmaps mark the trimmed ends for the owner's own view but public embeds still render them, deliberately — see [Fitness File Storage](fitness-file-storage.md#security-and-privacy)

### API Compatibility

- ✅ **Mastodon API v1/v2** — Compatible with Mastodon client applications (including iOS clients); statuses, accounts, and media endpoints are fully Mastodon-compatible
- ✅ **Mastodon-compatible status actions** — Favourite, reblog, bookmark, pin, context, history, translate, and relationship endpoints
- ✅ **Quote posts API (Mastodon 4.5)** — `quoted_status_id` + `quote_approval_policy` on `POST /api/v1/statuses`, the `quote` sub-entity and `quote_approval` on the Status entity, `GET /api/v1/statuses/:id/quotes`, `POST /api/v1/statuses/:id/quotes/:quoting_status_id/revoke`, `PUT /api/v1/statuses/:id/interaction_policy`, and the `posting:default:quote_policy` preference / `source[quote_policy]` on `update_credentials`; quote support is advertised to clients via `api_versions.mastodon: 7` on `GET /api/v2/instance`; the effective policy is clamped to the post's audience — a followers-only post is quotable only when its policy is `followers` (by accepted followers), a direct post only by its author
- ✅ **Granular OAuth scopes** — Fine-grained scope enforcement and client-credentials app tokens
- ✅ **Search** — Search accounts, hashtags, and statuses via `/api/v2/search` (status search backed by a full-text index)
- ✅ **Lists** — Create and manage timeline lists, their members, replies policy, and exclusive flag. Members are accounts you follow or have requested to follow, plus yourself, as in Mastodon ≥ 4.2: `POST /api/v1/lists/:id/accounts` accepts your own account without a follow, and a `following=true` account search — `GET /api/v1/accounts/search` or `GET /api/v2/search` — includes you, as Mastodon's do. An account you have only requested to follow joins the list at once, but its posts reach the list only once the request is accepted, which also brings in the posts of theirs this server already has; a rejected or withdrawn request removes it from your lists, as unfollowing does. Adding an account you neither follow nor have requested to follow is a `422` and an id that names no account a `404`, as in Mastodon
- ✅ **Filters** — Keyword/status filters via `/api/v2/filters` with notification filtering, plus the deprecated Mastodon v1 `/api/v1/filters` (and `/api/v1/filters/:id`) compatibility shim served as a view over the same v2 filter storage
- ✅ **Reports** — Submit reports against accounts and statuses via `/api/v1/reports`. With `forward` set, a report of a remote account is also sent to that account's server as an ActivityPub `Flag` from the instance actor. A `Flag` that another server sends to the shared or a personal inbox (Mastodon "forward to remote server", Misskey, Pleroma/Akkoma, GoToSocial) becomes a local report in the admin reports queue, as in Mastodon: it must name a local, non-suspended account (one report is filed per local account named, each with only that account's local statuses; reports about remote accounts are not accepted), the reporter is the sending actor (usually the other server's instance actor), the category is `other`, the comment is capped at 5000 characters, a reporter on a domain block with `reject_reports` is ignored, and a redelivery does not file it twice (identical reports from one server collapse into one while the first is unresolved)
- ✅ **Admin moderation** — Moderate accounts and reports via the Mastodon admin API (`/api/v1/admin/accounts`, `/api/v2/admin/accounts`, `POST /api/v1/admin/accounts/:id/action`, the approve/reject/enable/unsilence/unsuspend/unsensitive state actions, suspended-first `DELETE`, and the reports workflow `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/admin/reports[/:id]` + `assign_to_self`/`unassign`/`resolve`/`reopen`), enforced instance-wide (sign-in, token acceptance, ActivityPub 410, inbound-inbox drops, timeline filtering, forced-sensitive creation), with per-actor moderation controls and a reports queue in the admin area (`/admin/accounts/:id`, `/admin/reports`)
- ✅ **Markers** — Save and restore per-timeline read positions
- ✅ **Announcements** — Read active instance announcements via `GET /api/v1/announcements`, dismiss them with `POST /api/v1/announcements/:id/dismiss`, and react with `PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/announcements/:id/reactions/:name` (the name must be a unicode emoji or an enabled custom emoji's shortcode, and an announcement carries at most eight distinct reactions, as in Mastodon; anything else is a `422`); active announcements surface for signed-in users as a header icon beside Refresh on the home timeline (with a dot and a count in its name while any are unread; no icon when there are none) that opens a popover under the header, and admins manage them in the admin area (`/admin/announcements`) backed by `/api/v2/admin/announcements`; an announcement is for every served domain or for one (`domain`), and a visitor sees the every-domain ones plus those of the domain they are on
- ✅ **Endorsements** — Feature accounts on your profile
- ✅ **Account notes & preferences** — Private per-account notes and client preferences
- ✅ **Profile entity** — `GET`/`PATCH /api/v1/profile` return the Mastodon 4.6 `Profile` entity (raw `display_name`/`note`/`fields`, nullable `avatar`/`header`, and the appearance preferences `avatar_description`/`header_description`/`hide_collections`/`indexable`/`show_media`/`show_media_replies`/`show_featured`/`attribution_domains`); PATCH accepts and persists those appearance params on the actor's settings, while `PATCH /api/v1/accounts/update_credentials` still returns `CredentialAccount`
- ✅ **Featured tags API** — `/api/v1/featured_tags` endpoints plus the Mastodon 4.4 `POST /api/v1/tags/:tag/feature` and `POST /api/v1/tags/:tag/unfeature` aliases (both returning the `Tag` entity with the `featuring` flag), backing the profile featured-hashtags feature
- ✅ **Follow suggestions** — friends-of-friends suggestions via `GET /api/v2/suggestions` (plus deprecated `GET /api/v1/suggestions`), with per-account dismissal via `DELETE /api/v1/suggestions/:account_id`
- ✅ **Trends** — local trending hashtags via `GET /api/v1/trends/tags` (plus deprecated `GET /api/v1/trends`) and trending statuses via `GET /api/v1/trends/statuses`, both computed live from the last seven days of public local activity; `GET /api/v1/trends/links` intentionally stays an empty list (preview cards are stored, but a personal server computes no trend ranking over them)
- ✅ **Followed hashtags** — Follow and unfollow hashtags and view a followed-tags timeline
- ✅ **Mutes** — Mute and unmute accounts
- ✅ **WebFinger** — Actor discovery via `/.well-known/webfinger`, including the `http://ostatus.org/schema/1.0/subscribe` remote-follow template
- ✅ **Remote follow** — Both directions of the fediverse remote-follow handshake: `/authorize_interaction?uri=…` lets a signed-in local user follow an account another server sent them to, and a logged-out visitor viewing a local profile can enter their own handle to be redirected to their home server's follow page (resolved server-side via `GET /api/v1/remote-follow`)
- ✅ **NodeInfo** — Instance metadata at `/.well-known/nodeinfo` (NodeInfo 2.0 and 2.1)
- ✅ **OAuth Authorization Server metadata** — At `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`
- ✅ **Instance legal documents** — A terms of service and a privacy policy written in Markdown in Admin › Instance (or pinned by `ACTIVITIES_TERMS_OF_SERVICE` / `ACTIVITIES_PRIVACY_POLICY`), rendered the way Mastodon renders them (raw HTML shown as text, no images, `%{domain}` filled in) and served by `GET /api/v1/instance/terms_of_service` (plus `/:date`) and `GET /api/v1/instance/privacy_policy`, which return 404 while their document is unset, and linked from `/api/v2/instance` `configuration.urls`. When either is set, signing up requires agreeing to them; a Mastodon API registration always requires `agreement`, as Mastodon does
- ✅ **Health check** — Unauthenticated `GET /health` liveness probe returning `{"status":"UP"}`
- ✅ **Browser Web Vitals (opt-in)** — A sampled share of page loads (`ACTIVITIES_WEB_VITALS_SAMPLE_RATE`, off by default) reports LCP, CLS and INP to `POST /api/v1/web-vitals`, which writes one structured log line per metric with a route template and device class only; see [Browser Web Vitals](./architecture.md#browser-web-vitals)
- ✅ **oEmbed provider** — `GET /api/oembed` returns rich embed metadata for this instance's public/unlisted status pages, discoverable via the `application/json+oembed` `<link>` emitted on every status page
- ✅ **Notification policy** — Filtered-notification policy via `GET|PUT|PATCH /api/v2/notifications/policy`, plus the deprecated `GET|PUT|PATCH /api/v1/notifications/policy` alias serving the legacy `filter_*` boolean entity
- ✅ **CSV export and import** — `GET /api/v1/exports/<file>.csv` for `following_accounts`, `lists`, `muted_accounts`, `blocked_accounts`, `blocked_domains` and `bookmarks`, in Mastodon's exact formats (`following_accounts` newest follow first). `POST /api/v1/imports` (multipart: a file up to 5 MiB, `type` of `following`, `blocking`, `muting`, `domain_blocking`, `bookmarks` or `lists`, `mode` of `merge` or `overwrite`) runs a background import of up to 20,000 rows in resumable slices, with one running import per actor; `GET /api/v1/imports` and `GET /api/v1/imports/:id` report progress and failed rows. Backend only, there is no settings UI yet
- 📖 **[Mastodon API compatibility reference](./mastodon-api-compatibility.md)** — Intentional divergences (e.g. OAuth tokens that expire after about a week unused), unplanned endpoints, and activities.next-only extensions

### Infrastructure

- ✅ **Background jobs** — Async processing via Upstash QStash or Google Cloud Tasks (with synchronous fallback)
- ✅ **Email sending** — SMTP, Resend, or AWS SES (optional; notification delivery is best effort)
- ✅ **Docker support** — Official minimal container image at `ghcr.io/llun/activities.next` (SQLite default; modular workspaces for PostgreSQL and external queues)
- ✅ **Vercel deployment** — Deploy as a serverless Next.js application
- ✅ **Federation controls** — Admin allow/block rules, import for domain blocks, and allowlist mode
- ✅ **Multiple domains** — Serve profiles on more than one domain from one server. **Admin › Domains** lists the primary (`ACTIVITIES_HOST`), the `ACTIVITIES_TRUSTED_HOSTS` entries (always on) and the domains an admin added; adding one checks that DNS, TLS and the proxy's Host header reach this server, saves it as Off, and **Turn on** re-checks before trusting it. Each domain has its own sign-ups (open or closed, allowed email addresses, and whether existing users can add profiles there; someone who joined through another domain's sign-ups can add a profile on the primary or an `ACTIVITIES_TRUSTED_HOSTS` domain only while that domain's own sign-ups would admit them, or once they have a profile there): the sign-up page says "Join <name>" (the domain's name, else the server's) and creates the first profile on the domain you visit, `/api/v1/instance`, `/api/v2/instance` and NodeInfo report that domain's registrations, and a domain with closed sign-ups says so on its landing page. Each domain can also have its own identity and policies: a name, description, contact email, terms of service and privacy policy, each "Same as server" (Admin › Instance) until set. Visitors to that domain see them on the sign-up page, at `/terms-of-service` and `/privacy-policy`, and in `/api/v1/instance`, `/api/v2/instance`, `/api/v1/instance/extended_description`, the Mastodon terms and privacy endpoints and NodeInfo; the primary always uses the server's own. The visited domain's name shows in page titles, the web app manifest, sign-in, the landing page, passkey and 2FA labels, and in emails (wordmark, links and From name). Rules, announcements, features and reports are per domain too: each domain's page has Rules (its own rules after the server rules), Announcements, Features (it can turn off Fitness, Explore and Messages that the server has on) and Reports (a link to the reports queue filtered to that domain); Admin › Announcements picks the domain an announcement shows on, and Admin › Reports can filter by domain.
- ✅ **Database-backed server settings** — Edit instance identity, registrations, post/poll/upload limits, and federation policy from the admin area, resolved `env → database → default` (an environment variable wins and locks the field). See [Environment Variables](environment-variables.md#database-backed-server-settings-env--database--default)
